This week we are working on

Week 7 is our teamwork week. The athletes spend most of the session in relays, which means they are not just running fast, they are running fast while someone else is counting on them.

That changes the whole task. A child who can sprint happily on their own suddenly has to judge when to go, track where their teammate is, and hand something over without losing speed. It is genuinely hard, and it is why we teach it properly instead of just handing out batons and hoping.

We finish with overarm throwing for accuracy. Quieter work, but it builds the same underlying thing.

The session, start to finish

Warm up
Four station circuitSkipping, pogo jumps, push ups, squats, walking lunges, planks and squat jumps.
Main block
Relays and teamworkThrow and Go Relay, then a Circular Relay in teams of four with batons and four changeover zones.
Skill
Overarm throw for accuracyTechnique practice with tennis balls, then a points scoring target game.

Why it matters

Two things are being built here, and honestly, neither of them looks like athletics from the sideline.

The first is awareness. In a relay a child has to watch a teammate coming towards them and start moving at exactly the right moment. Go too early and the handover falls apart. Go too late and they throw away the speed they just built. That judgement is the same one they need on a footy field or a netball court.

The second is sequencing. A good overarm throw runs bottom to top: feet, then hips, then torso, then arm. Running and jumping use that same order. A throw is just the easiest place to learn it, because the ball tells you straight away whether you got it right.

What to notice at home

Give them a ball in the backyard and ask them to hit something. Then watch their feet, not their arm.

What we are practising this week is turning side on to the target and stepping towards it with the opposite foot. If they throw square on and it is all arm, that is the bit we are working towards. If they turn and step, that is worth telling them you noticed.

The words we use in session

  • Pogo jumps: bouncy toes, see if you can bounce higher.
  • Lunges: long stride, knee to the ground, drive the arm.
  • Throwing: point at the target, then take the ball back past your ear.

Same words at home means they only have to learn them once.

Ask your child

"What happens in a relay if you start running too early?"

"Which is harder, running fast or handing over without slowing down?"
